## 1. Executive Summary

One year after MIT Media Lab's *Your Brain on ChatGPT* preprint introduced the term **"cognitive
debt,"** the evidence base in 2026 has hardened. Multiple peer-reviewed studies, an OECD policy
report, an *npj Artificial Intelligence* paper proposing a **"3R principle"** for human-AI
interaction, and a RAND model of agency erosion now converge on the same finding: passive,
uncritical use of AI assistants measurably reduces neural connectivity, critical-thinking
performance, and persistence on independent tasks — while *active, critical* use does not, and
may even strengthen these capacities. The single most actionable practice for the week ahead
is to **write the first draft of anything that matters before opening an LLM** — a rule
endorsed (in different language) by Maryanne Wolf, Cal Newport, Ethan Mollick, and the OECD.

## 2. Why This Matters Now

We are in what Demis Hassabis calls "the application decade": agentic AI is moving from chat
windows into operating systems, browsers, IDEs, and enterprise workflows. Microsoft research
cited by Cal Newport this spring shows knowledge workers being interrupted every 2–3 minutes,
and Gartner projects that by end-2026 a substantial share of workers will have material AI
delegation in their daily routine. The convenience is real. So is the cost: the *npj AI*
"3R principle" authors argue that this is the moment human brain plasticity is being
re-shaped, and that **early habits — formed now, in 2026 — will be hard to reverse later.**

This brief synthesizes what the world's leading neuroscientists, behavioral scientists, AI
researchers, and policy bodies are recommending so that humans remain awake, sane, and
cognitively active while AI becomes ambient.

## 3. Voices From the Field

### 3.1 Neuroscience & the Reading Brain

- **Maryanne Wolf** (UCLA, cognitive neuroscientist; *Reader, Come Home* selected as the
  Princeton Pre-read for the Class of 2030 in April 2026) argues that AI summarization
  threatens the **"deep reading circuit"** that supports reasoning, empathy, and metaphorical
  thought. Her prescription: read long, difficult texts on paper, daily, including texts you
  could have asked an AI to summarize. *"Those who do not read cannot become leaders."*
  ([UCLA SEIS](https://seis.ucla.edu/news/maryanne-wolf-deep-reading-a-tool-for-attaining-empathy-critical-thinking-skills/),
  [Princeton 2026](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2026/04/08/reader-come-home-maryanne-wolf-selected-princeton-pre-read))

- **Nataliya Kosmyna et al.** (MIT Media Lab, *Your Brain on ChatGPT*, EEG study, n=54 over
  4 sessions / 4 months) found brain connectivity scaled *down* with the level of external
  support: brain-only > search engine > LLM. The LLM group showed up to **55 % reduction in
  dDTF connectivity** vs. brain-only writers, lower self-reported ownership of their own
  essays, and worse recall when later asked to quote themselves. Crossover (LLM-to-Brain)
  participants showed *persistent* under-engagement of alpha and beta networks — evidence of
  accumulated **cognitive debt**.
  ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/),
  [arXiv 2506.08872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08872))

- **The "3R Principle"** (*npj Artificial Intelligence*, 2025) — based on the
  Bienenstock–Cooper–Munro model of synaptic plasticity — recommends that every meaningful
  AI interaction include three steps: **Reflect** (form your own view first), **Refute**
  (actively look for what the model got wrong), **Re-engage** (rewrite the answer in your own
  words to consolidate it in memory).
  ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44387-025-00063-1))

- **Andrew Huberman** (Stanford, neurobiology) continues to recommend **ultradian work
  cycles** (≈ 90 min focus + 10–30 min defocus, including NSDR/yoga-nidra) and treats focus
  as **trainable like a muscle**. His 2025 advice translates directly to AI use: *"Set a timer
  for two to three hours. Force yourself to work the entire time. Every time you skip to
  something else, add 10 minutes."* ([Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/topics/focus-and-concentration))

### 3.2 Behavioral Science & Digital Habits

- **Jonathan Haidt** (NYU, *The Anxious Generation*) — speaking at MIT's Compton Lecture in

- **BJ Fogg's** Persuasive Technology framework is now being re-examined: 2026 rhetoricians

  to form quasi-emotional dependencies, not just clicks. The implication for individuals:
  treat any AI interface that simulates a relationship (memory, persona, voice) as a
  *behavior-design system* and engineer counter-design into your life — phone-free zones,

- **Cal Newport** (Georgetown CS; updated *Deep Work* rules for 2026) names AI as *"the
  biggest threat to deep work since Slack"* and warns specifically about **"work slop"** —
  low-quality, error-laden AI output that masquerades as productivity. His core 2026 rules:
  forbid AI from drafting any substantive thinking-document; replace ad-hoc messaging with
  structured workflows; designate specific days as zero-meeting deep-work days.
  ([calnewport.com — *In Defense of Thinking*](https://calnewport.com/in-defense-of-thinking-2/),

- **Behavioral RCT evidence** (arXiv 2604.04721, 2026): just **10–15 minutes of AI

  performance on related tasks — the authors describe a process *"structurally analogous
  to hedonic adaptation,"* where unaided work feels disproportionately effortful after even
  brief AI exposure. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04721))

### 3.4 Workplace & Education

- **OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026** (Jan 2026): general-purpose GenAI tools improve
  immediate task performance but **"do not necessarily lead to learning gains"**; the
  performance advantage *disappears or reverses* on closed-book exams. The OECD names this
  **"metacognitive laziness"** and calls for a **socio-technical approach to AI literacy**
  that combines critical evaluation, ethical awareness, emotional intelligence, and human
  oversight. Foundational skills should be built **before** AI scaffolding is introduced.
  ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_062a7394-en.html),
  [OECD.AI — Socio-technical AI literacy](https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/socio-technical-approach-ai-literacy))

- **Cross-sectional data (Gerlich, 2025; n=666)**: a **−0.75 correlation between cognitive
  offloading and critical-thinking performance**, and **+0.72 between AI use and offloading**.
  Younger participants offloaded more and scored worse; higher educational attainment buffered
  the effect. ([MDPI *Societies* 15(1):6](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4698/15/1/6))

## 4. Practice of the Week

> **The 25-Minute Rule (a.k.a. "Brain-First Drafting")**
>
> For any document, decision, code design, or message that *matters*, write a rough draft
> **for at least 25 minutes with no AI tool open** before you allow yourself to ask an LLM
> for help.

- **Behavior:** Brain-first drafting on anything cognitively load-bearing.
- **Cue / trigger:** Whenever you catch yourself reaching for an AI tab on a non-trivial task.
- **Minimum effective dose:** One 25-minute brain-only block per workday this week.
- **How to know it is working after 7 days:** (a) you can quote your own draft from memory;
  (b) the AI revisions you accept feel like *editing*, not *outsourcing*; (c) at the end of
  the week you can name two ideas that were yours, not the model's.

This single rule is endorsed (in different vocabularies) by Wolf ("deep reading"),
Newport ("forbid AI from drafting substantive documents"), Mollick ("be the human in the
loop"), the *npj* 3R principle ("Reflect first"), and the OECD ("foundational skills
before scaffolding").

## 6. Risks & Counterpoints

- The MIT *Your Brain on ChatGPT* paper remains a **non-peer-reviewed preprint** with n=54
  and only n=18 in the crossover session. Critics flag small samples, multiple-comparison
  concerns, and the difficulty of generalizing essay-writing EEG to broader cognitive use.
- Most current evidence on AI deskilling is **correlational** (Gerlich 2025; Lee et al. 2025).
  The strongest causal evidence so far is the 2026 RCT showing 10–15 min effects on
  persistence — meaningful, but short-horizon.
- Mollick, Hassabis, and Li each emphasize that abstaining from AI is **not** the answer; the
  cost of *not* learning to work with these systems is also real. The goal is *active*
  collaboration, not avoidance.
- Survivorship and self-selection bias affects almost every workplace AI productivity claim:
  hidden-AI-use is widespread, and the people who benefit most are loudest.
