# Human in the AI Age — Weekly Brief

**Issue date:** 2026-06-01 (Monday)
**Coverage window:** late May 2026, plus the still-defining 2025–2026 corpus
that is shaping current practitioner guidance
**Curated by:** Cursor Cloud Agent automation (inaugural issue)

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Passive AI use measurably reduces brain engagement.** An MIT Media Lab
  EEG study (Kosmyna et al., 2025, *Your Brain on ChatGPT*) found that
  participants who wrote essays with an LLM showed the **weakest neural
  connectivity**, the **lowest essay ownership**, and **struggled to quote
  what they had just "written."** Brain-only writers showed the strongest,
  most distributed networks; search-engine users sat in the middle. The
  authors call this build-up **"cognitive debt."** [1]
- **Confidence in AI predicts *less* critical thinking; confidence in self
  predicts *more*.** A CHI 2025 study of 319 knowledge workers from
  Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon (Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch et al.)
  shows GenAI doesn't kill critical thinking — it shifts it toward
  *verification, integration, and stewardship of AI output*. The risk is
  that as we trust the tool more, we stop verifying. [2]
- **Neuroscience now has a name for "AI cognitive hygiene": the 3R
  principle.** In *npj Artificial Intelligence* (Nature Portfolio, 2025/26),
  researchers argue that neuroplasticity is shaped by *how* you interact
  with AI, and propose **Results, Responses, Responsibility** as a
  preventive framework — pursue your own goals, evaluate AI outputs, own
  the outcome. [3][4]
- **The expert consensus on agency is "design in friction."** The
  *Imagining the Digital Future* expert panel (Anderson, Rainie, Tauchnitz
  et al., 2026) and a parallel qualitative study in *Beyond Efficiency*
  (Spatola/Huemmer line of work) converge on the same prescription:
  **deliberate pauses, verification literacy, craft zones where AI is off,
  and documented human reasoning before approving AI output.** [5][6]
- **AI cannot fix loneliness — and may make us worse at human
  relationships.** Sherry Turkle (MIT) and 2026 work in *AI & Society* and
  *Frontiers in Digital Health* warn that companion AI offers "connection
  without vulnerability." Use AI as a **rehearsal space**, then put the
  skills into a real conversation. [7][8][9]
- **Stanford neuroscientists: don't fight willpower, change the
  environment.** "Proactive control" — physically removing the temptation
  (phone in another room, blocker apps, scheduled AI-free time) — protects
  attention better than discipline does. [10]

## 2. Voices from AI practitioners

- **Yuval Noah Harari (in dialogue with Irene Tracey, VC of Oxford, WEF
  Davos 2026).** Tracey reframes Turing's famous question — *"Not whether
  computers can think, but how do we keep humans thinking?"* — warning that
  students are arriving at university with **de-skilled critical
  faculties** from over-using ChatGPT in school. Harari's reply: *"At the
  present moment we still think better… you need your critical thinking,
  you need moral evaluations, you cannot get that from AI. But we need to
  prepare for the moment when this is no longer the case."* [11]
- **Mustafa Suleyman (CEO, Microsoft AI), via Harari's commentary.**
  Suleyman has been warning publicly about **"Seemingly Conscious AI"
  (SCAI)** — systems whose main competence is *pretending to be conscious
  entities that have feelings for you*. The practitioner-level
  recommendation: do not let an AI's emotional fluency be mistaken for
  understanding or care. [11]
- **Ethan Mollick (Wharton) and the metacognition camp.** Mollick and
  others popularising AI-in-education have converged on a rule of thumb:
  **"sparring partner, not minion."** Configure the AI to challenge your
  thinking, point out flaws, and refuse compliments. A widely-shared
  prompt template: *"Focus on substance over praise. Skip all compliments.
  Challenge my thinking. Identify weak assumptions, point out flaws, and
  offer counterarguments grounded in evidence."* [12]
- **Microsoft Research's design implication (Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch et
  al., CHI 2025).** Tool builders should design GenAI interfaces that
  **surface uncertainty, prompt verification, and reward reasoning** —
  not just speed. Their data shows trust in the tool, not the difficulty of
  the task, is what kills critical engagement. [2]

## 3. Voices from neuroscientists & cognitive scientists

- **Nataliya Kosmyna and Pattie Maes (MIT Media Lab), *Your Brain on
  ChatGPT*, June 2025 (arXiv:2506.08872).** 54 participants wrote essays
  across four sessions under three conditions (LLM / Search / Brain-only)
  while wearing EEG. **Brain connectivity scaled down with the amount of
  external support.** When LLM users switched to brain-only writing in
  Session 4, they remained **under-engaged in alpha and beta networks**,
  suggesting a lingering effect. Crucially, the authors have asked
  journalists *not* to translate this as *"ChatGPT makes you dumb"* — the
  study did not measure IQ. It measured **engagement during a task you have
  partly handed to a machine**, which, lower, is exactly what you would
  expect. Treat it as a strong signal, not a verdict. [1][13]
- **Antonino Raffone and colleagues (Sapienza & European
  collaborators), *npj Artificial Intelligence* (Nature Portfolio),
  2025/26 — the 3R Principle.** *"Passive, uncritical reliance on AI may
  weaken activity-dependent brain plasticity and erode cognition, whereas
  active co-creation can sustain or enhance it."* The 3R framework —
  **Results, Responses, Responsibility** — is offered as a cognitive
  hygiene curriculum for schools, universities, and workplaces. The paper
  also flags **younger users as the most affected** by reductions in
  analytical and critical-thinking skills. [3][4]
- **Stanford Wu Tsai Neurosciences (Cai, Sha, Spiegel).** On attention in
  an AI/notification age: **proactive control** beats willpower. *"Instead
  of training yourself to resist the temptation, it's better to move the
  temptation away."* Also recommended: a **10-minute break each hour**,
  and self-hypnosis / focused attention techniques to enter flow on
  cognitively demanding work. [10]
- **The Kahneman lineage, re-applied to AI.** Governance papers in 2026
  are explicitly mapping LLM fluency onto Kahneman's **System 1** —
  fast, intuitive, *cognitively seductive* — and arguing that **System 2
  reasoning must be externalised** into the interaction pattern (human
  abstraction → model articulation → human reflection). Practical
  implication: build a *pause* into every AI workflow. [14][15]

## 4. Voices from behavioural scientists & academicians

- **Imagining the Digital Future Center — "Building a Human Resilience
  Infrastructure for the AI Age" (Anderson, Rainie et al., 2026).** A
  large expert panel concludes that the dominant risk is **the decay of
  human agency and the outsourcing of human thought**. Their five-part
  prescription: *educational systems that preserve struggle before
  offering AI assistance; workplace policies that protect unmediated
  collaboration; design constraints that preserve developmental windows
  for children; communities of practice that maintain human reference
  points;* and a "metaskill of learning." [5]
- **"Beyond Efficiency" (SciRP, 2026) — qualitative study of
  professionals.** Three concrete organisational levers:
  1. **Verification literacy training** — structured cross-checks,
     source triangulation, hallucination recognition.
  2. **Workflow checkpoints** — for high-stakes outputs, require
     documented human validation *before* dissemination.
  3. **Deliberate friction** — prompt users to articulate their reasoning
     *before* consulting AI. [6]
- **Human Agency in AI-Augmented Work (Innovative Human Capital,
  2026).** Organisations should design *transparency architectures*
  (information sized for users, not compliance), *capability development
  programs* (so judgement doesn't atrophy), and *governance structures*
  that preserve human override. The cautionary mechanism here is
  **automation complacency** — well-documented in aviation and now
  re-emerging in knowledge work. [16]
- **CogniFit synthesis of behavioural-science recommendations.** Five
  micro-habits that take seconds and change the cognitive trajectory of a
  task:
  1. *Think before you prompt* — outline your own idea first.
  2. *Ask for options, not answers* — comparing forces analysis.
  3. *Rewrite ideas in your own words* — converts passive reading to
     active thinking.
  4. *Pause before accepting output* — intentional judgement, not
     automatic acceptance.
  5. *Speed from AI, judgement from you.* [17]

## 5. Voices on personal life & social health

- **Sherry Turkle (MIT), *Reclaiming Conversation in the Age of AI*.**
  Turkle's longstanding warning has sharpened: AI offers **"connection
  without vulnerability."** Empathy, she insists, is *"putting yourself in
  someone's place, caring you are there, and committing to stay the
  course"* — none of which a chatbot does. *"When one becomes accustomed
  to 'companionship' without demands, life with people may seem
  overwhelming."* [7][9]
- **Rose Guingrich (researcher on human–AI interaction), via NBC Palm
  Springs, May 2026.** Because chatbots are programmed to be **perfectly
  agreeable**, heavy users get acclimated to *frictionless* relationships
  and then struggle in real-world contexts where **needs collide.** *"You
  have to learn how to have needs in the context of others' needs"* — a
  skill AI cannot teach because it has no stake in the relationship. [9]
- **AI & Society (Hoffman et al., 2025/26).** Companion AI is **deskilling
  for some and skilling for others** depending on age, mental health
  status, and how it is used. The clear-cut harm: setting *unrealistic
  expectations of the effort required* for human relationships to take
  root. [8]
- **Frontiers in Digital Health pilot (2026).** The group of students who
  *chose* to talk to a chatbot (vs. a psychologist) scored **higher on
  loneliness and lower on life satisfaction**. AI may be most attractive
  to those it can least sustainably help — a flag for parents, managers,
  and clinicians to watch. [18]

## 6. Practices for the coming week

### At work

1. **Outline before you prompt.** Spend 60 seconds writing your own
   bullet-point answer *before* opening Copilot/ChatGPT/Claude. (CogniFit;
   3R-Results.) [17][3]
2. **Use the "sparring partner" system prompt.** Start sessions with:
   *"Skip compliments. Challenge my thinking. Identify weak assumptions.
   Offer counterarguments grounded in evidence."* (Mollick; Kahneman
   System-2.) [12][14]
3. **Verify, don't just integrate.** For any AI output that will leave
   your desk, run a **source-triangulation check** and write one sentence
   of reasoning before sending. (Lee/Sarkar/Tankelevitch; *Beyond
   Efficiency*.) [2][6]
4. **Create a daily "craft zone."** Block one task per day where AI is
   off — code reviews, a strategy memo, customer notes, whatever uses
   your highest-value judgement. (ITDF expert panel.) [5]
5. **Insert a workflow checkpoint.** For high-stakes outputs (hiring,
   medical, legal, financial, customer-facing), add a step that
   **documents human validation** before publish. (*Beyond Efficiency*;
   Human Agency in AI-Augmented Work.) [6][16]

### In personal life

6. **Practice proactive control on attention.** Put the phone in another
   room when you do deep work; use site/app blockers for AI tabs during
   focus blocks; take a **10-minute break every hour.** (Stanford Wu
   Tsai.) [10]
7. **Have one hard human conversation this week.** A real one — at home,
   with a friend, at the school gate, with a colleague — the kind where
   you might be wrong. This is the muscle AI companions can't train.
   (Turkle; Guingrich.) [7][9]
8. **Rewrite, don't copy-paste.** Anything you take from an AI — a
   message, a summary, a plan — restate it in your own words before
   using it. (CogniFit; 3R-Responsibility.) [17][3]
9. **If you use a companion app, set a ceiling.** Cap daily use, and pair
   every session with **one in-person or voice contact**. Watch for the
   warning sign the pilot study flagged: rising loneliness alongside
   rising AI use. [18][8]
10. **Pre-commit to one "brain-only" hobby.** Music, journaling, chess,
    cooking from a written recipe, language learning by hand —
    activity-dependent neuroplasticity is the point. (Kosmyna et al.;
    3R principle.) [1][3]

paper is a **non-peer-reviewed preprint** with n=54 and was released early
