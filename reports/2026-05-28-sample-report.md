## 1. Why this report exists

We are living through the largest cognitive-tool transition in human history. In the
last 36 months, generative AI has moved from novelty to default — embedded in our
email, IDE, browser, phone, car, and (increasingly) our children's classrooms. The
short-term productivity wins are real. The long-term cost to attention, memory,
judgment, and agency is now showing up in peer-reviewed data.

This weekly report curates the most rigorous thinking from **AI researchers,
neuroscientists, behavioural scientists, and academics** on a single question:

> **How do we stay awake, curious, capable, and humanly _alive_ while AI takes
> over more of our everyday tasks?**

## 2. The headline finding of the week

**Neural cost of AI assistance is now measurable — and it lingers.**

A four-month EEG study from **MIT Media Lab** (Kosmyna, Hauptmann, Maes et al.,
2025, _"Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt"_) tracked 54
participants writing essays under three conditions: Brain-only, Search Engine,
ChatGPT. Key results:

| Metric                                          | Brain-only | Search | ChatGPT |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| Brain-network connectivity (alpha + beta bands) | Strongest  | Medium | **~55% lower** |
| Could quote a sentence they had just written    | ~89%       | ~71%   | **~17%** |
| Self-reported ownership of the essay            | Highest    | Medium | Lowest  |
| Time-to-complete                                | Slowest    | Medium | **60% faster** |

The most important — and least talked-about — finding came in **session 4**, when
the ChatGPT group was switched back to brain-only writing. Their neural
engagement did **not** rebound. The authors call this **"cognitive debt"**:
delegating thinking to AI appears to leave a residual under-engagement that
persists after the tool is removed.

A follow-up cross-sectional study of 666 participants (Gerlich et al., 2025)
reported a strong negative correlation (**r = −0.75**) between cognitive
offloading to AI and measured critical-thinking ability.

**Take-away:** AI use is _use-dependent_, not inherently harmful. The danger lies
in **passive offloading** — accepting outputs without engaging your own
prefrontal cortex.

## 3. What the world's best practitioners are recommending

Synthesised from this week's reading across AI research, neuroscience, and
behavioural science.

### 3a. Daniel Kahneman (behavioural economics / cognitive psychology)

- **"Wherever there is judgment, there is noise."** Algorithms beat human
  intuition in structured, repeatable tasks — use them there.
- For **unstructured, high-stakes** judgment, use **decision hygiene**: break the
  decision into independent dimensions, score each one separately, and only
  combine at the end. Do _not_ collapse the whole choice into a single intuitive
  vibe — that is exactly where AI can quietly take over.
- Run a **pre-mortem** before big calls: "It is two years from now and this
  decision was a disaster. What went wrong?" This re-engages System 2 _before_
  AI's confidence becomes contagious.

### 3b. Stuart Russell (UC Berkeley, AI safety)

- Design (and demand) **provably beneficial AI** — systems that are explicitly
  uncertain about human preferences and that ask for clarification rather than
  optimising hard for a guessed goal.
- Russell's rule of thumb (echoed by the Center for Humane Technology): for
  every dollar spent on making AI more powerful, **~25 cents** should be spent
  on steering, safety, and governance. Apply the same ratio personally — for
  every hour you spend leveraging AI, spend ~15 minutes auditing what it
  changed in your work and your habits.

### 3c. Yuval Noah Harari (historian / philosopher, WEF Davos 2026)

- AI is the **first tool that is also an agent** — it can learn, create, and
  manipulate language. Once it controls the language that organises human life
  (law, finance, religion, news), it controls more than a tool ever has.
- Defensive practice: **keep one domain in your life entirely AI-free** — a
  hobby, a relationship, a craft, a journal. Harari recommends 2 hours of daily
  silence/meditation; the goal is to retain a self that AI has not shaped.

### 3d. Andy Clark (philosopher of mind, "extended mind" thesis)

- Humans have _always_ been hybrid thinkers — notebooks, slide rules, Google.
  AI is the next layer. The question is not "use or refuse" but **what kind of
  coupling** you build.
- Clark calls the new discipline **"extended cognitive hygiene"**: stay an
  _active_ partner — question, refine, push back, refuse — rather than a
  passive recipient. Treat AI's first answer the way you'd treat a stranger's
  opinion overheard at a bar: interesting, possibly wrong, never final.

### 3e. Nataliya Kosmyna / Pattie Maes (MIT Media Lab, lead authors of the EEG study)

- The **highest-performing group** in their data was **Brain-to-LLM**:
  participants who wrote unaided first, then brought the AI in to refine.
- Operational rule: **"Think first, prompt second."** Even 60 seconds of your
  own thought before invoking an AI dramatically changes neural engagement
  patterns and recall.

### 3f. Cal Newport (Georgetown, computer scientist, _Deep Work_)

- AI is creating a paradox: tasks are individually faster, but **time spent in
  uninterrupted focused work has dropped ~9%** among AI-heavy users.
- Newport's protocol for the AI era:
  - **Time-block** non-negotiable deep-work sessions; AI is _not_ invited.
  - **Batch** AI interactions into dedicated "Audit Blocks" and "Agent
    Orchestration" windows.
  - Use a **shutdown ritual** at end of day — AI works 24/7 and will
    psychologically pull you to do the same.
  - Apply the **craftsman approach** to every new AI feature: adopt it only if
    its impact on what you actually care about clearly outweighs the
    distraction cost.

### 3f. Majid Fotuhi (neurologist, Johns Hopkins, neuroplasticity)

The brain stays plastic — you can _grow_ hippocampus and prefrontal cortex into
old age. Six daily habits with the strongest evidence base:

1. **Mediterranean-style diet** (olive oil, fish, vegetables, nuts, legumes).
2. **Aerobic exercise** — raises BDNF, the protein that grows neurons.
3. **Effortful learning** — new skills, new languages, instruments. _AI cannot
   do this for you._
4. **Sleep** (7–9 hours) — glymphatic clearance of metabolic waste.
5. **Meditation** — lowers chronic cortisol, increases cerebral blood flow.
6. **Sense of purpose** — Richard Davidson's group at Wisconsin finds purpose
   is _the strongest_ psychological predictor of long-term cognitive health.

### 3g. Nate Kornell (Williams College, learning science)

- **Desirable difficulty** is the secret of durable learning. AI removes
  difficulty — which feels good and is exactly the wrong thing for learning.
- Practical: deliberately use **"spacing"** (revisit material across days) and
  **"interleaving"** (mix old and new) when learning anything new — and do the
  retrieval _before_ asking AI to summarise.

### 3h. Shaw & Nave (Wharton, 2026) — extending Kahneman

A new framework: **Tri-System Theory**.

- **System 1**: fast, intuitive (human)
- **System 2**: slow, deliberate (human)
- **System 3**: external, algorithmic (AI)

The new failure mode is **"cognitive surrender"** — uncritically letting System
3 override System 2. The skill of our era: knowing _when_ deferring is rational
and when it is abdication.

## 4. Best-practice playbook — distilled to 10 rules

A consensus checklist drawn from the above sources. Copy this into your
notebook, your team wiki, or your child's bedroom wall.

1. **Think first, prompt second.** Always form a rough view in your own head
   before opening a chat window.
2. **One AI-free domain.** Keep at least one important area of life
   (journaling, a sport, an instrument, parenting) untouched by AI.
3. **Brain-only blocks.** Calendar 60–90 min/day where the AI tab is closed.
4. **Treat AI as hot sauce, not ketchup.** Use it deliberately on specific
   tasks, not on every task.
5. **Question every output.** Default skepticism: _"What is missing? Is this
   actually true? Where would this be wrong?"_
6. **Take notes by hand.** Manual encoding survives the cognitive-debt effect.
7. **Pre-mortem big decisions.** Imagine the failure before you decide.
8. **Audit weekly.** Once a week, look back: which tasks did AI take from me?
   Which of those did I _want_ it to take? Course-correct.
9. **Protect sleep, exercise, sun, real conversation.** The substrate matters
   more than the software.
10. **Find purpose.** The single most robust predictor of long-term cognitive
    health. AI cannot give it to you.
