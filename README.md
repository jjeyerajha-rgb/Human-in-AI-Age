# Human-in-AI-Age

> A weekly research brief on how to stay **alive, sane, and cognitively active** while
> AI tools become ambient in our work and personal lives. Synthesizes the most relevant
> recent recommendations from named subject-matter experts in **AI**, **neuroscience**,
> and **behavioral / cognitive science**.

## What this repo is

This repository is the home of an automated weekly research report ("Human in the AI Age")
generated by a Cursor Cloud Agent.

Every Monday at **03:30 UTC** (cron `30 3 * * 1`), the agent:

1. Pulls the latest thoughtful research and advice from leading practitioners and academics —
   AI researchers (Hassabis, Li, Russell, Bengio, Mollick, Karpathy…), neuroscientists
   (Wolf, Huberman, Gazzaley, the *npj AI* "3R principle" authors…), and behavioral
   scientists (Haidt, Fogg, Wood, Newport…), plus reports from OECD, MIT Media Lab,
   Stanford HAI, RAND, and the International AI Safety Report.
2. Synthesizes the findings into a structured Markdown brief.
3. Commits the brief to `reports/weekly/YYYY-MM-DD.md` on the development branch.

## The brief always answers

1. **What new evidence emerged this week** about how AI tool use affects human cognition,
   agency, or wellbeing?
2. **What do the world's best practitioners recommend** across four domains:
   neuroscience, behavioral science, AI research, and workplace/education?
3. **What is the single most actionable practice** a knowledge worker should adopt this week?
4. **What is the always-on cognitive hygiene checklist** to self-audit daily?
5. **What are the credible counterpoints**, so the brief stays intellectually honest?

## Editorial principles

- **Named experts only.** Every recommendation is attributed to a specific researcher,
  practitioner, or institution.
- **Primary sources.** Each brief links peer-reviewed papers, institutional reports, and
  original talks. Preprints are flagged as such.
- **One actionable practice per week.** Cognitive change comes from habit, not novelty.
- **Counterpoints required.** Every brief includes a "Risks & Counterpoints" section to
  resist hype in either direction.
- **Not advice.** The briefs are research synthesis, not medical or psychological advice.
